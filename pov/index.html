What I'd change about most brands' social.

Four years of planning and running paid social for national brands — most recently a $20.5M plan across six channels in two countries — taught me that brand social is usually run as a media plan with content bolted on. The evidence points the other way. Here are the changes I would make in an in-house seat, each with the test that convinced me.

The short version

  1. Let the test decide who makes the creative. Creators won by a mile for a product; the brand film won by 24% on tune-in; AI-generated creative came last.
  2. Buy intent, not interests. Search-keyword targeting beat interest targeting on Pinterest and cut CPA 40% on Reddit.
  3. Give every placement a job. Pulse held attention; TopView drove clicks. Judge each on its job.
  4. Optimize to the signal that moves consideration. Engaged visits out-lifted three other signals in a four-cell test.
  5. Measure lift, not likes. A lift study or holdout on every launch that matters.
  6. Run organic and paid as one system. The reason I want an in-house seat.

Let the test decide who makes the creative

What most brands do: decide by belief — either "creators are more authentic" or "the brand film is more premium" — and never run the head-to-head.

What I'd change: run creator, brand and AI-generated creative head to head on matched spend for every launch that matters, because the answer flips with the objective. For a product measured on attention and efficiency, creator content won by a mile. For an entertainment launch measured on tune-in, the brand's own creative won by 24% — and AI-generated creative finished last. Build the creator engine anyway; it is the cheapest way to get variants. But let the numbers decide what leads.

Buy intent, not interests

What most brands do: prospect on interest and lookalike audiences on every platform, because that is what the platforms default to.

What I'd change: treat social search as search. Build keyword sets from the brand's own search-console queries, comments and customer language; put them to work on Pinterest and Reddit first; move interest-only prospecting budget toward intent, and let lookalikes do retargeting's job, not discovery's.

Give every placement a job — and judge it on that job

What most brands do: compare premium placements on one CPM line and keep the cheapest.

What I'd change: assign each placement a job before the buy. On TikTok, Pulse buys attention next to content people chose to watch; TopView buys reach and clicks. Read each on its own metric, and stop paying attention prices for traffic or traffic prices for attention.

Optimize to the signal that moves consideration, not the cheapest proxy

What most brands do: optimize consideration campaigns to landing-page views or ad recall because they are cheap and easy to hit.

What I'd change: define what an engaged visit means on the brand's own site first — time, depth, a meaningful action — and make that event the optimization goal. Then prove it with a lift study, not a dashboard.

Measure lift, then report like a strategist

What most brands do: report platform metrics — reach, views, engagement rate — and call the campaign a success when they go up.

What I'd change: a lift study or a holdout on every launch that matters, and a one-page readout that says what the media changed in people's heads or behaviour. Platform metrics diagnose; lift decides.

Run organic and paid as one content system

What most brands do: organic lives with the community team, paid lives with media, and the two share a logo.

What I'd change: organic is the cheapest creative test a brand will ever run — every post is a read on what the audience wants more of. Paid should amplify what organic already proved, comments should feed the brief, and one calendar should hold both. No number for this one; it is the reason I want an in-house seat rather than another agency desk.

The five tests above were run from the paid side of the wall. Each would have been faster and cheaper with organic and community in the same room: creator picks from the comments, keyword sets from real questions, and lift studies designed around what the calendar was already saying.

How I'd run this in-house: the first 90 days

The point of an in-house seat is that strategy, creative and execution sit together. This is the order I'd do it in.

Days 1–30 · Learn and audit

  • Audit organic and paid together: what each channel is actually doing, and what it's being judged on.
  • Pull the brand's own intent data — search queries, comments, support tickets — into keyword sets.
  • Stand up a creator roster with clear briefs, rights and turnaround times.
  • Map the build process; find the four-day step.

Days 31–60 · Test the big bets

  • Matched-spend creative test: creator vs studio on identical media.
  • Intent vs interest prospecting on Pinterest and Reddit.
  • Define the engaged-visit event with analytics; design the signals test around it.
  • Put a Buildcheck-style QA loop in front of every launch.

Days 61–90 · Decide and scale

  • Lift study on the first big launch; holdouts on everything else.
  • Assign placements their jobs and rewrite the reporting to match.
  • Shift budget to what the tests said — creator-led creative, intent-led prospecting.
  • A one-page monthly readout leadership can act on.
