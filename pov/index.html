What I'd change about most brands' social.

Four years of planning and running paid social for national brands taught me that brand social is often built around the media plan, with content adapted to fit it. My strongest results point to a different approach: let audience behavior, creative and platform context shape the strategy from the start.

These aren't universal rules. They're the changes I'd test first for a brand, each grounded in a real campaign result.

The six changes

Each one: what I usually see, what I'd change, and the test that convinced me. The goal comes first every time; it decides what "working" means.

1

Let the test decide who makes the creative

What I usually see: creative format gets chosen by assumption: creators for authenticity, brand films for polish, AI for scale. Those assumptions often replace the head-to-head test.

What I'd change: test creator, brand and AI-generated creative under matched media conditions, then assign each format to the outcome it proves it can drive. In an equal-spend Meta test, creator content won on view rate, click-through rate, cost per interaction and shares, while brand creative led on CPM and follows. In a separate tune-in test, creator-boosted ads outperformed both brand and AI creative.

Creators earn the stop and the share; the brand earns the follow.

2

Buy intent, not just interests

What I usually see: prospect on interest and lookalike audiences on every platform, because that is what the platforms default to.

What I'd change: treat social search as search. Build keyword sets from the brand's own search queries, comments and customer language; put them to work on Pinterest and Reddit first, and write the creative's first line in the same words. Broad targeting still earns its place when the goal is pure reach; once the goal is consideration or action, lead with intent, and let lookalikes do retargeting's job, not discovery's.

40% lowerCPA on Reddit

Launched a national brand on Pinterest with search-keyword targeting, which beat interest targeting on completion rate, view rate and engagement. The same approach on Reddit cut cost per acquisition 40% against the prior spring and summer campaign.

3

Give every dollar a job, and grade it on that job

What I usually see: compare placements on one CPM line and optimize to whichever event is cheapest.

What I'd change: name the job first (attention, clicks or consideration), then buy, optimize and grade against it. On TikTok, Pulse sits next to videos people chose to watch, so it buys attention; TopView opens the app, so it buys clicks. On Meta, the event you optimize to decides what you get back.

4

Measure lift, not likes, then report like a strategist

What I usually see: report platform metrics (reach, views, engagement rate) and call the campaign a success when they go up.

What I'd change: name the one outcome the campaign exists to move (awareness, consideration, tune-in or sales). Read it with a holdout on every launch that matters (it costs nothing but a slice of reach) and a lift study when the budget allows, and report it on one page leadership can act on. 5

Put AI in the workflow, not the feed

What I usually see: AI aimed at what the audience sees, while the slow, manual work behind every launch stays as it was.

What I'd change: test AI creative like any other creative, and don't give it the feed until it wins. Point it first at the checking, building and reporting that slow a team down, with a person signing off on every launch.

6

Run organic and paid as one content system

What I usually see: organic lives with the community team, paid lives with media, and the two share a logo.

What I'd change: organic is the cheapest creative test a brand will ever run, because every post shows what the audience wants more of. Paid should amplify what organic already proved, comments should feed the brief, and one calendar should hold both. It's the change I most want to own next.

Since 2019, before agency life, I ran organic social through two internships and a campus publication: content calendars, graphics, blogs and weekly performance reporting. Several tests above could have started from what organic already knew: which hooks land, which creators people trust, and the words they search with.

How I'd run it

The first 90 days, with strategy, creative and execution in the same room. This is the order I'd do it in.

Days 1 to 30

Learn and audit
  • Agree on each campaign's goal and how we'll know it worked, then audit organic and paid against it.
  • Set content pillars and a posting cadence from what the audience already responds to organically.
  • Map the build process; find the four-day step.

Days 31 to 60

Test the big bets
  • Stand up a creator roster, then run creator vs studio creative on identical media.
  • Intent vs interest prospecting on Pinterest and Reddit.
  • Make community a routine: comments and DMs answered daily, recurring questions fed into the brief.

Days 61 to 90

Decide and scale
  • Lift study on the first big launch; holdouts on everything else.
  • One calendar for organic and paid, with paid amplifying what organic already proved.
  • Shift budget to what the tests said, in a one-page readout leadership can act on.
